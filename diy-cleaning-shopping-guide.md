## 1. The SLES Swap

The original recipes used sodium C14-16 olefin sulfonate (~40%). SLES 70% is a much more concentrated gel, so you need only **about 57% of the weight** and make up the difference with water.

| Product | Olefin sulfonate (before) | SLES 70% (now) | Water (before → now) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Car shampoo (500 g) | 75 g | **43 g** | 327 g → **359 g** |
| Dish liquid (500 g) | 90 g | **51 g** | 314 g → **353 g** |
| Laundry detergent (1 kg) | 150 g | **85 g** | 656 g → **721 g** |

Every other ingredient stays the same.

## 3. Handling SLES 70% Gel

SLES behaves differently from the liquid olefin sulfonate, so a few habits help:

- **It is thick and sticky.** Warm the container in a bowl of warm water to make it pour more easily, and use a spoon to scrape it onto the scale.
- **Dissolve it slowly.** Stir it into warm water gently until no lumps remain, then add the other ingredients. Stirring too fast makes foam.
- **It thickens a lot with salt.** In the shampoo and dish liquid, add salt a pinch at a time, since SLES can thicken quickly and then thin out again.
- **Keep the pH at 6 or above.** SLES slowly breaks down in acid, so don't overshoot with citric acid.
- **In the laundry detergent,** add the SLES after the builders (citrate, washing soda, gluconate) have dissolved.

## 6. Shopee Sourcing Tips

- **Search these keywords:** `SLES gel 1kg`, `coco betaine`, `decyl glucoside`, `AEO-7`, `sodium citrate`, `sodium gluconate`, `liquid germall plus` or `phenoxyethanol`.
- **Consolidate shops.** Buy from one or two suppliers that sell soap-making or DIY detergent raw materials. This cuts shipping costs.
- **Check the product details.** Look for the concentration (SLES should say 70%), the grade, and the net weight, and read recent reviews.
- **Start with a small batch.** If you aren't sure about a supplier, order the smaller size first.
- **Store everything sealed**, away from heat and sunlight, and label each container with the date you opened it.
