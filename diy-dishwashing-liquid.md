# DIY Dishwashing Liquid

*A gentle, foamy, pH-balanced hand dishwashing liquid you can make at home with cosmetic-grade ingredients.*

---

## Overview

Store-bought dishwashing liquid is a blend of **surfactants** (which lift grease and food), a **foam booster**, a **thickener**, and a **preservative**. This recipe uses the same building blocks, balanced to be effective on grease while staying reasonably kind to your hands.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Yield** | about 500 g |
| **Active surfactant** | roughly 12-13% |
| **Target pH** | 6 to 7 |
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Time** | About 30 minutes, plus 1 day resting |

---

## Recipe

| Ingredient | Amount | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Distilled water | ~314 g (about 63%) | Base |
| Sodium C14-16 olefin sulfonate (~40%) | 90 g (18%) | Main degreasing surfactant |
| Cocamidopropyl betaine (~30%) | 50 g (10%) | Foam booster, mildness |
| Decyl glucoside (~50%) | 25 g (5%) | Gentle cleaner, helps with grease |
| Glycerin | 10 g (2%) | Hand-feel, slickness |
| Preservative (e.g., a phenoxyethanol-based one such as Liquid Germall Plus) | 3-5 g (0.5-1%) | Prevents bacteria and mold |
| Sodium gluconate or EDTA *(optional)* | 1 g (0.2%) | Helps in hard water |
| Salt (sodium chloride) | 5-8 g (1-1.5%) | Thickens |
| Citric acid solution | A few drops | Adjusts pH |
| Fragrance and dye *(optional)* | A few drops | Cosmetic |

> **Tip:** SLES (~70%) can replace the olefin sulfonate. Use about 50 g instead of 90 g, and increase the water by the difference.

All ingredients are available from soap-making and cosmetic ingredient suppliers, or online.

---

## Equipment

- [ ] Digital scale (0.1 g accuracy)
- [ ] Glass or plastic beakers and a stirring rod or spoon
- [ ] pH test strips (a 5-9 range works well)
- [ ] Gloves and goggles
- [ ] Clean bottles with pump or flip-top caps

> **Safety:** Concentrated surfactants irritate skin and eyes. Wear gloves and goggles while mixing, and keep the concentrate away from children.

---

## Process

1. **Weigh** the distilled water into a beaker.
2. **Add the surfactants** one at a time (olefin sulfonate, then betaine, then decyl glucoside), stirring slowly to avoid excess foam.
3. **Mix in** the glycerin and the chelating agent, if using.
4. **Add the preservative**, then fragrance and dye if desired.
5. **Check the pH.** You're aiming for about 6 to 7. Add citric acid solution drop by drop to lower it.
6. **Thicken with salt.** Dissolve a pinch at a time in a spoonful of the mixture, then stir it back in. The liquid thickens gradually. Too much salt will make it thin out again, so stop once it reaches the consistency you like.
7. **Rest it** for a day so the bubbles settle, then check the thickness once more and bottle it.

---

## How to Use It

| Method | Amount |
|---|---|
| Directly on a sponge | A few drops to a small squirt |
| Sink or basin of water | About 5 ml (1 tsp) per 4-8 L |
| Heavy grease (pans, baking trays) | Apply a little undiluted, let it sit a minute, then scrub |

---

## Caveats

- **Don't skip the preservative.** Without one, a water-based mix will grow mold and bacteria within days.
- **Not for dishwashing machines.** Hand dishwashing liquid foams heavily and can overflow a dishwasher.
- **Never mix with bleach or ammonia.** Mixing cleaning products can release toxic gases.
- **Thickness varies.** Different batches of surfactants thicken differently, so make small test batches first and adjust the salt.
- **Rinse well.** Always rinse dishes thoroughly, especially cups, bowls, and baby items.
- **Shelf life.** With a proper preservative, expect around 6 to 12 months. Discard it if it changes smell, color, or texture.

---

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Too thin | Not enough salt | Add salt in small increments |
| Gets thinner after adding salt | Too much salt | Add a little water or a bit more betaine |
| Cloudy or has bubbles | Mixed too fast | Let it rest for a day |
| Weak foam | Hard water or greasy dishes | Add the chelating agent, or use a bit more |
| Dry hands | pH too high or too little glycerin | Lower the pH toward 6, or add a little more glycerin |

---

## Bottom Line

This formula gives you a workable, good-smelling dishwashing liquid for a fraction of what specialty brands cost, but a store-bought bottle is still less effort for everyday use. Make it if you enjoy the process or want control over the ingredients.
