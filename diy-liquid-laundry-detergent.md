# DIY Liquid Laundry Detergent

*A mild, low-cost liquid laundry detergent built from the same kinds of cosmetic-grade ingredients used in the car shampoo and dishwashing liquid recipes, with a few additions that laundry needs.*

---

## Overview

Laundry detergent works differently from dish soap or car shampoo. On top of surfactants, it needs a **builder** to soften water and keep dirt from settling back onto fabric, and a **slightly alkaline pH** (about 8 to 9) so it can lift body oils and everyday soil.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Yield** | about 1 kg (roughly 1 L) |
| **Active surfactant** | about 14% |
| **Target pH** | 8 to 9 |
| **Difficulty** | Easy to moderate |
| **Time** | About 45 minutes, plus 1 day resting |
| **Loads per batch** | About 15-20 |

---

## Recipe

| Ingredient | Amount | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Distilled or deionized water | ~641 g (about 64%) | Base |
| Sodium C14-16 olefin sulfonate (~40%) | 200 g (20%) | Main cleaning surfactant |
| Alcohol ethoxylate (e.g., C12-15 pareth-7 or laureth-7, ~100%) | 60 g (6%) | Nonionic surfactant for oils and greasy stains |
| Sodium citrate | 50 g (5%) | Builder, softens water |
| Sodium carbonate (washing soda) | 20 g (2%) | Raises pH, boosts cleaning |
| Glycerin or propylene glycol | 20 g (2%) | Keeps the mix stable and clear |
| Preservative (e.g., a phenoxyethanol-based one such as Liquid Germall Plus) | 5 g (0.5%) | Prevents bacteria and mold |
| Fragrance *(optional)* | 3-5 g (0.3-0.5%) | Scent |
| Dye *(optional)* | A few drops | Cosmetic |
| Citric acid solution | A few drops | Lowers pH if it overshoots |

> **Tip:** SLES (~70%) can replace the olefin sulfonate. Use about 115 g instead of 200 g, and add the difference in water.

Ingredients are available from soap-making and cosmetic ingredient suppliers, or online. Washing soda is sold in supermarkets as a laundry booster.

---

## Equipment

- [ ] Digital scale (1 g accuracy is fine; 0.1 g for the preservative)
- [ ] Large glass or plastic beaker or bucket (at least 2 L)
- [ ] Stirring rod or long spoon
- [ ] pH test strips (a 5-9 range works, a 6-10 range is better)
- [ ] Gloves and goggles
- [ ] A clean 1 L bottle or jug with a pour spout

> **Safety:** Washing soda and concentrated surfactants irritate skin and eyes. Wear gloves and goggles while mixing, and keep the detergent away from children and pets.

---

## Process

1. **Dissolve the builders.** Warm the water to about 40 °C (comfortably warm, not hot). Stir in the sodium citrate and washing soda until fully dissolved, then let it cool slightly.
2. **Add the olefin sulfonate** slowly, stirring gently to avoid foam.
3. **Blend the alcohol ethoxylate with the fragrance** in a small cup first, then stir it in. The ethoxylate helps the fragrance dissolve. If it is waxy, warm the cup in hot water briefly.
4. **Mix in** the glycerin or propylene glycol.
5. **Add the preservative**, then dye if using.
6. **Check the pH.** You want about 8 to 9. If it is above 9.5, add citric acid solution drop by drop.
7. **Rest it** for a day so the bubbles settle. Stir gently, check the appearance, then bottle it.

---

## How to Use It

| Load | Amount |
|---|---|
| Normal load (4-5 kg) | 50-75 ml |
| Heavily soiled load | Up to 100 ml |
| High-efficiency (HE) machine | 30-40 ml |
| Pre-treating stains | Rub a little undiluted into the stain, wait 10 minutes, then wash |

Wash with warm or cool water. Use the detergent as directed on a small test load first.

---

## Caveats

- **Weaker than commercial detergent.** Store-bought products contain enzymes, optical brighteners, and polymers that are hard to replicate at home. Expect good results on everyday soil, but less on tough stains.
- **Not for wool or silk.** The alkaline pH can damage protein fibers. Use a gentler product for those.
- **It foams.** Olefin sulfonate foams a lot. In HE or front-loading machines, use the lower dose, or reduce the sulfonate and add water.
- **Don't skip the preservative.** Without one, a water-based mix will grow mold and bacteria within days.
- **Never mix with bleach or ammonia.** Mixing cleaning products can release toxic gases.
- **Test first.** Make a small batch and try it on a load of everyday items before committing to a full one.
- **Shelf life.** With a proper preservative, expect around 6 months. Discard it if the smell, color, or texture changes.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cloudy or separating | Too much washing soda or cold water when mixing | Dissolve the builders fully in warm water, or reduce the washing soda slightly |
| Too much foam in the machine | High-foaming surfactant | Use less detergent, or reduce the sulfonate in the next batch |
| Clothes feel stiff or look dull | Detergent residue | Use less, and add an extra rinse |
| Weak cleaning on greasy stains | Not enough nonionic | Increase the alcohol ethoxylate to 8-10% and reduce the water to match |
| Fragrance separates | Fragrance not dissolved | Blend it with the alcohol ethoxylate before adding |
| Thin, watery texture | Normal for this formula | Thickness doesn't affect cleaning, but a small pinch of salt can thicken it slightly |

---

## Bottom Line

This formula makes a workable, low-cost liquid laundry detergent for everyday clothes. For heavy stains, sportswear, or delicate fabrics, a commercial detergent with enzymes is still the more effective choice. Make it if you enjoy the process or want control over what goes into your wash.
